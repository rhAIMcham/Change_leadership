Recognising and responding to emerging risks

How can leaders recognise when an organisation is becoming vulnerable to changing circumstances, and what risks might emerge when existing practices or successes are no longer appropriate?

Saved!

Organisational risks are not always immediately obvious, particularly when established practices have previously delivered positive results. A significant risk can emerge when leaders become too comfortable with existing approaches and fail to recognise that the circumstances supporting their success are emerging.

Managing strategic and long-term risk

What should leaders consider when making strategic decisions that may provide immediate benefits but create longer-term organisational risks?

Saved!

Leaders need to consider both the immediate outcomes and the potential long-term consequences of their decisions. While reducing costs, improving efficiency or relying on external expertise may provide short-term advantages, these decisions can also create dependencies, reduce internal capabilities or limit future flexibility.

Organisational adaptability and the responsibility of leadership

What responsibility do leaders have for preparing their organisations to manage unexpected challenges or significant change?

Saved!

An organisation’s strengths can become vulnerabilities when leaders rely on them or assume that previous success will continue in a changing environment. Leaders have a responsibility to regularly examine whether their organisation’s capabilities, processes and business model remain relevant. This involves encouraging critical discussion, listening to staff and customers and questioning decisions that may have historically delivered success.

Balancing established practices with organisational change

What risks might arise from changing too quickly or maintaining existing approaches for too long?

Saved!

Organisational adaptability does not require completely abandoning established practices or continually restructuring an organisation. Leaders need to critically evaluate which processes continue to create value and which may be limiting future performance. Balancing respect for organisational history while remaining prepared to recognise that past success does not guarantee future relevance is crucial for navigating changing markets and customer expectations.

Staff participation and adaptability

To what extent should responsibility for organisational adaptability be shared between senior leaders and team members?

Saved!

Although the leadership team is responsible for establishing strategic direction, organisational adaptability is strengthened when staff are actively involved in identifying challenges and developing solutions. Team members often have direct knowledge about operational processes, customer expectations and workplace culture that may not be visible at an executive level. Meaningful consultation can therefore improve the practical implementation of change initiatives while building trust and commitment in an organisation.

Building a culture of continuous adaptability

What conditions need to exist for staff to feel confident challenging established practices and contributing to unfamiliar and potentially unsuccessful ideas?

Saved!

Organisational adaptability is most sustainable when it becomes part of the organisation’s everyday culture rather than an activity reserved for periods of significant change. LEGO’s emphasis on bravery, curiosity and focus illustrates how shared principles can guide behaviours without prescribing rigid processes or limiting individual contribution.

• Books for everyone

    Founded in 1971 as a single brick-and-mortar store, Borders was an international book retailer that transformed the book-buying experience for the customer. Its luxurious retail spaces invited readers to browse an extensive range of books, settle into an armchair and leisurely explore their selections before making a purchase. The stores became destinations in their own right, turning book shopping into an enjoyable weekend activity, an excursion or a social outing with friends.


    Borders also expanded its offering to include DVDs, CDs and other literary and entertainment-related merchandise, encouraging customers to spend more time in its stores. However, by the late 1990s, the emergence of online retailers presented a significant threat, challenging not only Borders’ market share but also established consumer behaviours. Online shopping offered greater convenience and allowed competitors to match Borders’ extensive book collection, by shipping directly from warehouses without the need to maintain large physical retail spaces.

  • Challenged by online shopping

    Borders made several unsuccessful attempts to establish itself in the online market, eventually outsourcing its online book sales to the newly established Amazon after a hesitant initial effort in the 1990s. By 2001, operating an online retail platform was still expensive and technically challenging, with Borders spending more than it was earning from online sales, making the partnership with Amazon seem to be a reasonable business decision. The arrangement allowed Amazon to meet investor expectations by selling under the established Borders name, while Borders could concentrate on its core business practice: providing an enjoyable in-store book-buying experience.


    However, the agreement was negotiated at a time when online retail was still relatively new, and its terms ultimately favoured Amazon. Amazon retained valuable customer data from online purchases and took ownership of the online sales, returning only a percentage to Borders. While this arrangement was initially beneficial, the rapid growth of online shopping gradually allowed Amazon to capture an increasing share of Borders’ potential market. Customers did not always require the browsing experience that Borders’ physical stores were designed to provide, and when purchasing last-minute gifts or replacement textbooks, it was more convenient to purchase these items online without investing in the shopping experience.


  • Missed the moment

    In 2008, Borders attempted to regain control of its online retail operations, but the years spent outsourcing to Amazon had left the company outpaced by its competitors. While Amazon had invested in improving its shipping capabilities, digital infrastructure and understanding of online consumer behaviour, Borders had committed substantial resources to expensive retail leases and physical book inventories. The launch of Borders.com was intended to help the company reclaim its online market share, but it quickly became apparent that Amazon’s sophisticated use of customer data, personalised recommendations and insights into purchasing behaviour gave it a considerable competitive advantage that Borders struggled to match.


    Borders continued to lose ground over the next three years before closing its doors in 2011. After a final unsuccessful attempt to sell the business, approximately 10,700 employees lost their jobs and hundreds of stores shut down, marking the end of Borders in the new digital age.

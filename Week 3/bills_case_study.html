    Origins

    Caption: Origins, 1993
  • Early 1990s

    A new kind of café

    In the early 1990s, 24-year-old Bill Granger opened his first café, Bills, in Sydney, introducing a fresh approach to dining that focused on simple food, a relaxed atmosphere, and fostering a shared community. A self-taught chef who had initially moved from Melbourne to study art, Granger discovered his passion for food while working as a waiter.
    Recognising Sydney’s vibrant morning culture and the opportunity to make dining out for breakfast more approachable, he built a simple café menu with corn fritters, scrambled eggs, and avocado on toast. The venue’s large windows, natural light and communal dining table encouraged customers to connect, creating a welcoming environment where eating out felt comfortable rather than formal or expensive.

    “I grew up in Melbourne, and when I moved to Sydney, I was shocked by its morning life. People were on the beach, walking through the park, owning the day. It felt very Australian, very optimistic. I think avocado on toast is optimistic.”
  • 1993

    Standing out

    At a time when Australia’s breakfast café culture was still developing, Granger’s holistic approach helped Bills stand out. He prioritised both the food and the overall customer experience, aiming to make every visitor feel valued and welcome. The communal table became a defining feature of the venue, encouraging social connection while improving service efficiency and seating in a small space.
    His success demonstrates how leaders can identify emerging opportunities, challenge established expectations and develop new approaches in response to people’s needs. By combining a clear vision with an understanding of changing customer tastes and practical business decisions, Granger established a distinctive café experience that would grow into a successful brand.

  • Late 1990s

    Growing empire

    Caption: Growing empire, late 1990s
  • 1996

    Expanding across Sydney

    By 1996, Bill Granger had expanded Bills to a second café in Surry Hills, followed by a third in Mosman before the end of the decade. The business attracted critical acclaim for its fresh approach to breakfast dining, while a list of celebrity clientele established Bills as a popular Sydney destination. Granger’s reputation for diligence and integrity also led to collaborations, television appearances and features in print media, raising the profile of both the chef and his growing business.

  • Partnership

    Creativity and financial discipline

    Behind this success was the partnership between Granger and his future wife, Natalie Elliott, a documentary filmmaker who brought valuable business and financial expertise to the operation. Together, they balanced the creativity and distinctive identity of the Bills brand with the financial management needed to support its growth.

  • 2000s

    International acclaim

    Caption: International acclaim, 2000s
  • 2000

    Sydney Food

    The 2000s marked a turning point for Bill Granger with the publication of his first cookbook, Sydney Food, in 2000. The book introduced his kitchen and personality to a wider audience, reflecting the Bills café philosophy of creating simple, well-made dishes using quality ingredients. It is also widely credited with recording avocado on toast as an official recipe, gaining Granger international recognition and contributing to his growing global influence.

  • 2002

    The Egg Master of Sydney

    Despite expanding into other food ventures, Bill Granger’s priority remained keeping consistent food and customer service standards at his venues. In 2002, Johnny Apple’s New York Times article titled The Egg Master of Sydney drew attention from across the Pacific.

    “I would stack Mr. Granger’s [eggs] up against all corners, fully confident that they would hold their own.”Johnny Apple, The New York Times, 2002

    Supported by Natalie Elliott, Granger continued to build the business, releasing his second cookbook, Bill’s Open Kitchen, in 2003, which would also go on to become a bestseller.

  • 2004

    Bill’s Food on screen

    In 2004, Bill’s Food aired in Australia. The series was well-received in Australia before reaching international audiences, attracting two million viewers during its UK screening and eventually being broadcast in more than 22 countries.
    This success demonstrated that Granger’s approachable cooking style and the welcoming philosophy behind Bills resonated with audiences around the world, solidifying his reputation as an international food personality.

  • 2005

    Simply Bill

    In 2005, Granger released his third cookbook, Simply Bill, further cementing his place in Australian kitchens. Through his books and television appearances, he continued to promote an approach to Australian food that focused on both ingredients and the experience of preparing and sharing meals.

  • 2006

    Television abroad

    Granger appeared on GMTV in June 2006 to present a week-long barbecue special filmed in the south of France. A second series of Bill’s Food was also released on various international networks.

  • 2007

    Bills goes to Japan

    In 2007, Bill Granger expanded the Bills brand internationally for the first time, opening a café in Kanagawa, Japan. This venture proved highly successful, attracting more than 230,000 diners before a second Japanese café opened in Yokohama in 2010.
    Alongside this international growth, Granger released his 2009 cookbook, Holiday, which explored more diverse flavours and reflected the brand’s evolving culinary influences.

  • 2010s

    Stardom

    Caption: Stardom, 2010s
  • 2011

    Granger & Co in London

    Bill Granger moved to London in 2010 and shifted his focus from television production to refocus on his restaurants, with the final season of Bill’s Food airing in 2010. In 2011, he entered the British market with Granger and Co, retaining signature dishes of ricotta hotcakes, creamy scrambled eggs and avocado toast.
    This expansion, while continuing to develop cookbooks, required significant commitment and adaptation to different food cultures, climates and locally available produce. Despite these challenges, Granger aimed to maintain the distinctive, relaxed and welcoming atmosphere associated with Bills in Sydney.

  • 2014

    Stepping back from the limelight

    2014 saw Granger continue the international expansion of Bills, opening cafés in Seoul and Hawaii, adding to his existing venues across Sydney, Japan and London. While this growth created exciting opportunities, it also increased the demands of managing a global business. Recognising his commitments to television and publishing were taking time and attention away from core operations, Granger decided to step back from the limelight.

    “I basically said no to everything. Ultimately [writing and filming] takes a lot of time and takes you out of your main business for a while. Being on TV is a completely different mindset. You’ve got to look after yourself. You’re a performer. It’s all about you. Whereas restaurants are not about you, they’re about customers, your staff. I found it very hard to do both. Restaurants need a lot of attention.”Bill Granger, 2020

    In the following years, Granger focused on refining Bills’ recipes and ensuring each international venue adapted to its local environment while maintaining the brand’s distinctive identity. Stepping away from the public spotlight allowed him to concentrate on the restaurants and their ongoing development, while also creating more space for his growing family.

  • 2020s

    Resilience

    Caption: Resilience, 2020s
  • 2020

    Australian Food and the pandemic

    In 2020, Granger released Australian Food, his first cookbook since 2013, reflecting on two decades of change in Australia’s food culture and the evolution of Sydney’s dining scene. The book celebrated the opportunities available to modern diners and the development of Australian cuisine since his first cookbook, Sydney Food, was published in 2000.
    However, the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted these plans, forcing hospitality businesses to navigate lockdowns and restrictions that limited the social dining experiences central to the Bills concept.

  • Recovery

    Coming back together

    As restrictions eased, customers returned to cafés, rediscovering the value of shared spaces. Familiar routines, and connecting with their communities.

    “Suddenly, we remembered how much we loved those things we used to take for granted when we were chasing the latest fashions. What was great was how everyone bounced back and came together to support each other. And people came running back for avocado toast.”

    While many hospitality businesses struggled during the pandemic, Bills’ diversified business model helped it withstand the disruption and emerge stronger.

  • Present

    Legacy

    Caption: Legacy, present day
  • Present

    Continuing the work

    Bill Granger passed away at the age of 54, leaving behind a successful international business and a lasting influence on Australian food culture. His wife and business partner, Natalie Elliott, has continued his work, guiding the business through a difficult period and maintaining its operation across Australia, Japan, South Korea and the United Kingdom. With 19 restaurants serving approximately 1.5 million people annually, Bills has demonstrated remarkable longevity in a competitive industry.
    Although its menus and venues have evolved, the business has remained grounded in Granger’s original philosophy of combining quality food with warmth, connection and a welcoming customer experience.

    “[Bill] was a trailblazer who did come at things in his own way. He wasn’t egocentric. He really understood what hospitality actually is.”Natalie Elliott
  • Legacy

    The Bill Granger Trailblazer Award

    Granger’s legacy extends beyond his restaurants to the broader hospitality industry. His approach demonstrated that hospitality is more than just about preparing food – it is also how people feel, connect and experience a place. To recognise his contribution, his family partnered with the Good Food Guide to establish the Bill Granger Trailblazer Award, celebrating individuals, teams and businesses that push the boundaries of Australian food while championing warmth, integrity and hospitality.

• Language learning

    Duolingo is a popular language-learning app that launched in 2011, built around making language education accessible, engaging and easy to fit into busy lives. Rather than relying solely on traditional teaching methods, Duolingo used gamification, short lessons, daily practice streaks and personalised exercises to encourage frequent use. Its approach differentiated the app from competitors by combining accessible learning with technology that adapted exercises to users’ needs. This focus on user engagement became central to Duolingo’s business model and brand identity, providing a foundation for future experimentation and growth.

  • Connecting to a new audience

    Duolingo later expanded its approach to marketing by transforming its green owl mascot, Duo, into a distinctive online personality. Through dramatic notifications, humorous in-app content and videos uploaded to social media websites, the company participated in online trends to connect with younger audiences. This strategy required the marketing team to rapidly respond to emerging opportunities, experiment with content and understand how to retain audience attention. The success of Duo demonstrated how an organisation could adapt its existing strengths to new platforms, turning advertising into content that audiences actively wanted to engage with. In 2022, Duolingo expanded its creative capabilities by acquiring animation study Gunner and developed partnerships with Netflix and other brands that strengthened its online presence.


  • AI-first pivot

    However, Duolingo faced a significant challenge in 2025 when its CEO announced that the company would become an ‘AI-first’ organisation. Although the company had already experimented with artificial intelligence to accelerate course development, this announcement drew significant criticism from users concerned about AI-generated learning content and the future value of human-led language education. Some users cancelled subscriptions or uninstalled the app entirely, showcasing a potential conflict between the organisation’s drive for efficiency and the expectations underpinning the brand. Duo had become a recognisable character whose popularity was facilitated by human creativity, which became a point of friction against an AI-driven company.

  • Recalibrating

    Although this change intended to improve productivity, it introduced new risks to customer trust, brand reputation and perceived value of a product. As a result, Duolingo temporarily removed videos from its social media accounts while considering its next steps, demonstrating the need to reassess decisions when unexpected consequences materialise.

Balancing organisational objectives with individual needs

How should leaders balance organisational priorities with the diverse needs of employees during significant change, particularly when some of those needs may conflict with the organisation’s objectives or available resources?

Saved!

Effective change leadership requires recognising that a decision can be strategically beneficial for an organisation while creating unequal impacts across its workforce. In the Optus case study, the new campus provided modern facilities and new opportunities while creating unequal impacts across its workforce. Leaders should avoid assuming that a change that benefits the majority will work equally well for everyone.

Adapting when an initial solution creates new challenges

What does the Optus case study reveal about the limitations of planning change in advance, and how should leaders respond when a solution to one problem creates new challenges?

Saved!

Organisational change rarely unfolds exactly as planned because decisions interact with infrastructure, resources, employee needs and circumstances that may be difficult to predict. The transport strategy demonstrates the importance of treating change as an ongoing process instead of a single decision followed by immediate implementation. Leaders should monitor the effects of their decisions, seek feedback from those affected, reassess assumptions and remain receptive to adjust their approach.

Identifying psychosocial hazards and encouraging employee feedback

How can leaders identify risks before and during organisational change, encourage honest feedback from reluctant employees and use this information to improve outcomes?

Saved!

Leaders need to recognise that the effects of change vary between employees according to their circumstances, responsibilities, resources and access to support. Longer commutes, increased travel costs, unfamiliar routes and difficulties accessing public transport were potential sources of stress that may not have been apparent when planning the change, but employee feedback allowed the organisation to develop target responses to these concerns. Feedback must lead to meaningful action, and leaders should communicate which changes can be made, and review if interventions are effective.

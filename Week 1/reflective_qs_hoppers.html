Types of organisational change

What type of organisational change can you identify in Hoppers’ approach to overhauling its online support system?

Saved!

This change affected multiple areas simultaneously, including technology, customer service processes, communication channels, team member responsibilities and the delivery of service. This could be an example of rapid transitional and transformational change as this has been a significant transition in how customers can access online services and support. However, it is also important to acknowledge the differences between genuine organisational change and cosmetic change.

Factors to consider before introducing change

What factors should have been considered before incorporating new technology into Hoppers’ processes?

Saved!

When understanding the purpose of change, it is crucial to consider the needs of customers and team members, and the organisation’s broader objectives. Considering that AI integration is an expensive, developing technology that requires practice to utilise effectively, other relevant factors could have been considering the issues customers would need assistance resolving, the reliability and limitations of the chatbot, the impact on agents, implementation costs, potential risks and how the change would resolve existing problems.

Success of the change

What was the initial problem Hoppers was trying to solve, and was it successful?

Saved!

The success of change is measured by how it addresses the original problem and creates meaningful value for customers, team members and the organisation. Using new technology in an organisation does not mean that an organisation has meaningfully adapted, especially when this new process risks alienation the organisation and their customer base. Hoppers may have been trying to address concerns over delays when resolving claims or other issues with the support staff, but their solution to these issues only generates customer frustration caused by the ineffective digital experience.

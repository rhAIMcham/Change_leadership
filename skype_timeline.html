• 2003 - 2005

    Early days

  • August 29, 2003

    Skype is launched

    The early 2000s was a period of rapid globalisation and technological development, facilitated by increased accessibility to the Internet and online networks. However, making long-distance phone calls was still an expensive and complicated process. Recognising the need for faster, cheaper communication, a small team of developers created Skype. This pioneering technology allowed for cheap long-distance calls for the first time, connecting families around the world.

  • December, 2004

    1 million users

    By the end of their first year, they had over a million simultaneous users online at peak. The small size of the company allowed them to improve features rapidly, and the free to use model allowed them to rapidly gain users and expand their reach.

  • 2005 - 2009

    Tenure at eBay

  • September, 2005

    Acquired by eBay

    Skype's life as a private company was short lived. After rocketing to popularity in Europe, Skype caught the attention of another early internet heavy weight, eBay. Skype was acquired by eBay for $2.6 billion in 2005. Up until this point Skype had primarily been used by people seeking to connect with family and friends or by small businesses, but eBay saw a different future for Skype. As then CEO Meg Whitman told The New York Times, the plan was to integrate skype into eBay’s online marketplace, allowing vendors and purchasers to call with one another. They also hoped this would enable Skype to grow in the business world, “particularly with transactions involving real estate, big-ticket purchases, and services that require detailed conversations.”

    Why did eBay want Skype?

    Beyond the headline price tag, the acquisition reflected eBay's bet that voice communication could reduce friction in online transactions — buyers and sellers negotiating big-ticket items over a call rather than back-and-forth messages.

  • December, 2005

    Video calling arrives

    Skype further cemented their place as an early internet juggarnaut with the introduction of video calling in 2005. For the first time, people were able to speak to family and friends overseas through video feed, connecting people around the world. This technology was so influential and successful that Skype's user count hit 54 million just as the company hit their secod birthday.

    Crucially, this video call technology also opened doors in the world of business. Communication with another office or team far away no longer needed to be impersonal or text only, as colleagues could call each other easily and reliably so long as they both had an internet connection.

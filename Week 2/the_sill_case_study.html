• Local house plants

    In 2012, Eliza Blank opened The Sill – a small plant shop to service her local community in New York. While many boutique plant shops sold curated plant-related homewares and rare plants, the Sill was an early innovator in the market and also offered horticultural education for their customers, including assistance in diagnosing, repotting and position plants for nervous first-time buyers.


    "I think the Sill helped make houseplants cool. In our industry, houseplants are regularly a top category.” Katie Dubow (President of Garden Media Group)


    As demand grew, the Sill expanded to offer online ordering in addition to more brick-and-mortar stores across the US. Blank then launched a plant subscription in 2018, with subscribers receiving a new plant each month, while her original New York City location expanded to serve other businesses across Manhattan. However, this success did not match Blank’s original vision, as she wanted to make the purchase of all kinds of plants more accessible to the buyer, rather than being restricted to smaller, apartment-oriented houseplants.

  • Indoors more

    The global pandemic proved fruitful for The Sill. Stuck inside, consumers were incentivised to purchase more plants to brighten their homes, leading to a peak year in retail sales in 2022. Simultaneously, ten stores were open across the country from New York to Los Angeles. Despite this success, Blank chose to move the business towards a fully online model in 2024. When the lease for the flagship store expired in 2022, she chose not to renew, believing that an online retail space could provide better business opportunities. This allowed Blank to achieve what she had always wanted: a comprehensive store for anything and everything green and outdoors, uninhibited by the restrictions of a physical storefront.


    “My ambition for this business was always to be a full solution, then I got caught up in the houseplant because it became this millennial obsession. Most brands, they’re comfortable with what they know, so they’ll just continue to act the same even as their customers age – or they’ll try and acquire the younger version of the customer.”

  • Online expansions

    The Sill had pre-emptively recognised the evolution of needs of their consumer base. The benefits of caring for a single houseplant are expanded to the benefits of being in the garden, investing in long term outdoor plant care. The Sill continues to offer judgement free advice for gardening basics and other resources, with a library of resources, workshops and a virtual consultation portal providing to resources curated by a familiar brand. In 2024, Blank stepped down as CEO and brought in Adam Smith, who had previously served as COO and then CEO of Fast Growing Trees, another ecommerce plant business. With experienced leadership, The Sill continues to grow and meet the needs of its customers.
